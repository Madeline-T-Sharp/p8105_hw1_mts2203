## Explaining `mean` Results

### What Works? What Doesn’t?

The mean calculations worked for the numeric variable (`vec_num`) and
the logical variable (`vec_log`). The mean calculations did not work for
the character variable (`vec_char`) and the factor variable
(`vec_fact`).

## Explaining `as.numeric` Results

### What happens? Why?

The function `as.numeric` converts values from non-numeric data types to
the numeric data type.

- **Logical vector (`vec_log`):** all instances of “TRUE” are converted
  to “1” and all instances of “FALSE” are converted to “0”. R reads
  these “1”s and “0”s as meaningfully numeric since they communicate the
  proportion of “TRUE” values.

- **Character vector (`vec_char`):** all 10 elements are converted to
  “NA” from their assigned character values. R cannot interpret
  arbitrary text as numbers. However, if a character contains a number
  as text, R converts this to its corresponding numeric value.

- **Factor vector (`vec_fact`):** all instances of “YES” are converted
  to “3”, all instances of “NO” are converted to “2” and all instances
  of “MAYBE” are converted to “1”. R converts factor variables into the
  numeric representations associated with their underlying integer
  codes. These integer codes are ordered based on the element’s level,
  e.g. “YES” = “3”; “NO” = 2; “MAYBE” = 1. As such, these conversions
  hold no meaningful numeric weight; they function categorically.

### Does this help explain what happens when you try to take the mean?

- Yes, this process communicates that to successfully take the mean of a
  variable, the variable must either have a numeric data type, or have
  the capacity to become meaningfully numeric. In this exercise, this
  includes:
  - the numeric vector, `vec_num`,
  - and the logical vector, `vec_log`.
